# Adventures of Bo, Ep.1 "The Mysterious Boop": v2 Treatment

## 1. What changed in the idea

v1's mystery had one engine: Bo tilts, it boops, repeated nine times. v2 keeps the title, the cast and the headphone reveal, but rebuilds the engine around two experiences almost everyone has had:

- **The chirp you can't find.** A tiny, intermittent beep (the smoke alarm at 2 a.m.) that seems to come from everywhere.
- **Searching for the glasses on your own head.**

**The boop is Bo's headphones warning of a low battery.** It plays *inside his ears*, so wherever he goes it is always "right here". That makes his searching inherently funny, and it gives the episode an ending you can act out (charging them) rather than one you have to explain.

**Dramatic irony instead of a twist.** In the first act the audience is shown, once and clearly, that the earcup ring blinks with every boop. Bo doesn't know. From then on every search, stakeout and theory is funnier because we're ahead of him. The characters still get their reveal, and it's a visual one: Bo sees himself blink in the reflection of Blu's sunglasses.

**Personality as method.** Each friend tries to solve the mystery the way only they would:

| Character | Method | What it says about them |
|---|---|---|
| **Bo**, thoughtful, curious | Stakeouts, careful listening, elimination | Takes small things seriously; dignified even when wrong |
| **Pip**, playful, unpredictable | Boops *everything* with its body to hear what sound it makes | Treats the world as a toy; joyful chaos |
| **Mimi**, warm, cheerful | Decides the boop is a shy little creature and tries to befriend it | Leads with kindness; gives the episode its heart |
| **Blu**, clever, observant | Triangulates the sound, then solves it with a mirror (his lenses) | Calm, precise, a little smug, and secretly loves to bounce |

## 3. Visual direction

- **World.** Bo's nook is a miniature attic room at night. Pitched ceiling beams, a round window with rain and a blue moon, warm brass lamp light, worn plank floor, woven rug, books, a mint-green twin-bell alarm clock (the hero suspect). Warm interior against cool exterior is the episode's colour script. Mood shifts: the cozy amber opening goes moonlit and suspicious in the investigation, gets chaotic in Pip's act, and returns to warm with the music.
- **Camera.** Real lenses, heights and angles: floor-level wides at 24 mm, profile mediums at 50 mm, 85–100 mm close-ups with shallow depth of field, an overhead for the triangulation, whip pans for the spin, a mirror close-up in Blu's lenses. Every move is motivated by a character's attention.
- **Acting.** Every action gets anticipation, overshoot and settle. Squash and stretch preserve volume. The fur lags, flares and **bristles** (puffs out on a fright, then slowly settles). That is the plush equivalent of facial acting, and v1 had none of it.

This sequence was chosen because it proves the four things v1 lacked in the least runtime: acting (lean, deflate, whip-turn, startle, deadpan), cinematography (seven distinct setups, lens changes, a whip pan), environment and light (moonlit night room, practicals, reflections), and comedic timing with sound (silence, sting, *boop*).

## 5. Production pipeline (v2)

1. **Assets.** Characters are 3D bodies with simulated fur strands (90k strands for Bo) generated from a locked spec. Accessories are modelled geometry. Every shot loads the same files, so they look identical from shot to shot.
2. **Rig.** A numpy deformation rig drives body, fur, eyes and headphones from one function: volume-preserving squash/stretch, bend, heading, spring-based overlap, fur inertia, flare and bristle.
3. **Animation.** Keyed per shot with anticipation, overshoot and settle; timing is checked first in a fast low-sample previs playblast.
4. **Render.** Cycles path tracing (real light, shadows, reflections, depth of field), denoised, AgX tone mapping.
5. **Sound.** Score written as MIDI and rendered through sampled orchestral instruments (FluidSynth); layered foley; the signature boop.
6. **Review.** Every shot is critiqued against this treatment before it is accepted.

## 6. Honest constraints

- **Compute.** 4 CPU cores, no GPU. The proof renders at 1280×536 (2.39:1) at roughly 20–30 s per frame and is shown letterboxed in 1080p. A studio would render the episode at 4K on a GPU farm; the same scene files scale to that unchanged.
- **Animation is keyed by code, not by an animator at a rig.** The acting principles are applied deliberately, but a professional animator's hand-polished arcs and spacing will still be better. Recommendation for the full episode: keep these assets and the treatment, and have a character animator (Blender or Maya) key the hero acting shots, with this pipeline handling fur, lighting and rendering.
- **Sound is mixed by measurement.** I can't listen to my own mix; a human listening pass is required before lock.

### What doesn't work yet
1. **Squints don't read.** The rig narrows the eye shape but has no eyelids, so "one eye squints harder" and the narrowed-eyes standoff (shot 7a) read as plain round eyes. *Fix:* fur-covered eyelid shells that close over the eye.
2. **Animation is keyed by code.** Timing and principles are deliberate, but spacing is even and arcs aren't hand-finessed; a character animator would make the lean, the deflate and the deadpan funnier.
3. **Bo's colour drifts between setups**: warm brown-charcoal under the lamp (shot 5), blue-grey under the moon (shot 9). It needs a unifying grade pass against the cast lock sheet.
4. **Shot 7b's foreground** (Bo's headband, defocused) reads as an abstract shape rather than over the shoulder.
5. **Resolution and samples** are set by 4 CPU cores: 1280×536 at 16 spp, denoised and upscaled. The finest fur detail is softer than a 4K GPU render would be.
6. **Scope:** only Bo is animated. Mimi, Pip and Blu are locked assets (`output/v2/cast_lock_sheet.png`) but their acting is unproven.
7. **I can't hear the mix.** Levels are measured, not listened to; a human listening pass is required.
